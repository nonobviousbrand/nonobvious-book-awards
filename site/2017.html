The 2017
Book Awards

In this third year of our program, we officially expanded to include a Longlist of an additional 15 books due to the quality of submissions we received — five winners, ten shortlist selections and the best business books of 2017.

Every year we select less than 10% of all the books we review to be included in our Non-Obvious Book Awards Longlist or Shortlist.

The 2017 Winners

The Best Non-Obvious
Books of the Year

Most Original · 2017 Winner

Win Bigly

by Scott Adams

Dilbert creator Scott Adams dissects the persuasion playbook behind one of the most unlikely campaigns in political history. A provocative field guide to influence in a post-fact world.

Buy AmazonBuy Local
Most Useful · 2017 Winner

Exactly What To Say

by Phil M. Jones

Phil M. Jones distills persuasion into a set of magic words: precise phrases that change how people respond to you in sales conversations, negotiations and everyday influence.

Buy AmazonBuy Local
Most Entertaining · 2017 Winner

Fifty Inventions That Shaped the Modern Economy

by Tim Harford

Tim Harford spins the stories of fifty unlikely inventions, from barbed wire to the barcode, that quietly built the modern economy.

Buy AmazonBuy Local
Most Shareable · 2017 Winner

Hit Refresh

by Satya Nadella

Satya Nadella’s inside account of transforming Microsoft by putting empathy at the center of one of the world’s most powerful companies.

Buy AmazonBuy Local
Most Important · 2017 Winner

Give Work

by Leila Janah

Samasource founder Leila Janah argues the surest way to end global poverty is not charity but work, and shows how giving work transforms lives and communities.

The Shortlist

Ten shortlist selections.

The finalists — narrowed from the longlist and revealed during the live announcement show.

Disrupt!
by James Bidwell

Springwise curator James Bidwell collects 100 of the most inspiring innovations from around the world, a global idea bank for anyone trying to spark their next move.

The Power of Moments
by Chip Heath and Dan Heath

Chip and Dan Heath reveal why certain brief experiences jolt, elevate and change us, and how to create more defining moments on purpose.

Unleashing the Innovators

Former P&G marketing chief Jim Stengel studies how smart companies partner with startups to recapture their founding energy.

The Unmade Bed

Buy AmazonBuy Local
Hit Makers

Derek Thompson’s science of popularity: why some products and ideas take off while equally good ones vanish. Distribution has never been explained better.

No Ego

Cy Wakeman brings hard data to a bold claim: workplace drama comes from ego, and leaders who stop indulging it reclaim enormous amounts of wasted energy.
