The 2019
Book Awards

An extremely competitive year with more books submitted and considered than any previous year — five winners, ten shortlist selections and a longlist of the best non-fiction books of 2019.

Every year we select less than 10% of all the books we review to be included in our Non-Obvious Book Awards Longlist or Shortlist.

The 2019 Winners

The Best Non-Obvious
Books of the Year

Most Original · 2019 Winner

Nine Lies About Work

by Marcus Buckingham and Ashley Goodall

Marcus Buckingham and Ashley Goodall take aim at the workplace’s most cherished orthodoxies, from culture to feedback to work-life balance, and explain what the evidence actually says.

Buy AmazonBuy Local
Most Useful · 2019 Winner

Writing to Persuade

by Trish Hall

Longtime New York Times op-ed editor Trish Hall spent years deciding which arguments worked. Here she shares what actually changes minds, and why facts alone never do.

Buy AmazonBuy Local
Most Entertaining · 2019 Winner

Junior

by Thomas Kemeny

Thomas Kemeny’s irreverent, genuinely useful guide to making it in advertising. The funniest career book we have ever honored.

Buy AmazonBuy Local
Most Shareable · 2019 Winner

Late Bloomers

by Rich Karlgaard

Rich Karlgaard pushes back on our obsession with early achievement and celebrates the quiet power of blooming on your own schedule.

Buy AmazonBuy Local
Most Important · 2019 Winner

Secondhand

by Adam Minter

Adam Minter follows our castoffs through the hidden global economy of secondhand goods, revealing what really happens to the things we give away.

The Shortlist

Ten shortlist selections.

The finalists — narrowed from the longlist and revealed during the live announcement show.

Alchemy
by Rory Sutherland

Ogilvy vice chairman Rory Sutherland’s gleeful case for magic over logic: why the irrational ideas are so often the ones that work.

Buy AmazonBuy Local
Audience of One
by James Poniewozik

New York Times TV critic James Poniewozik traces how television remade politics, and how one man learned to play the medium better than anyone.

Buy AmazonBuy Local
Invisible Women
by Caroline Criado Perez

Caroline Criado Perez documents the data gap at the heart of a world designed for men, from crash-test dummies to drug trials. One of the decade’s most important books.

Buy AmazonBuy Local
Medallion Status
by John Hodgman

John Hodgman’s wry meditation on fame, status and airline loyalty tiers. The funniest book ever written about being almost recognizable.

Buy AmazonBuy Local
Loonshots
by Safi Bahcall

Physicist-entrepreneur Safi Bahcall explains why organizations kill their wildest ideas, and the structures that let loonshots survive long enough to change the world.

Buy AmazonBuy LocalListen To Episode
Stop Being Reasonable
by Eleanor Gordon-Smith

Philosopher Eleanor Gordon-Smith tests how persuasion really works through six true stories of people changing their minds, and shows how rarely reason has anything to do with it.

Buy AmazonBuy Local
Scam Me If You Can
by Frank W. Abagnale

Frank Abagnale, the con artist behind Catch Me If You Can, turns his expertise to protecting readers from the scams targeting them right now.

Buy AmazonBuy Local
Range
by David Epstein

David Epstein’s influential case that generalists, not specialists, are primed to excel in a complex world. The book that made sampling widely respectable again.

Buy AmazonBuy Local
User Friendly
by Cliff Kuang with Robert Fabricant

Cliff Kuang reveals the hidden history of user-centered design, and how the ideas behind easy-to-use machines quietly became the grammar of modern life.

Buy AmazonBuy Local
Ultralearning
by Scott H. Young

Scott Young, who completed MIT’s computer science curriculum in a year on his own, shares the strategy of aggressive self-directed learning.
