The 2018
Book Awards

A year with twice as many entries and an expanded Longlist alongside the usual Shortlist — five winners, ten shortlist selections and the top business books of 2018.

Every year we select less than 10% of all the books we review to be included in our Non-Obvious Book Awards Longlist or Shortlist.

The 2018 Winners

The Best Non-Obvious
Books of the Year

Most Original · 2018 Winner

Winners Take All

by Anand Giridharadas

Anand Giridharadas’s unsparing look at how the global elite’s world-changing efforts often protect the very systems that created the problems. A book that reframed an entire debate about philanthropy.

Buy AmazonBuy Local
Most Useful · 2018 Winner

Factfulness

by Hans Rosling

Hans Rosling’s data-driven case that the world is better than you think, and a training manual for the ten instincts that distort how leaders read the world. Bill Gates called it one of the most important books he has ever read.

Buy AmazonBuy Local
Most Entertaining · 2018 Winner

The Formula

by Albert-László Barabási

Network scientist Albert-László Barabási reveals the universal laws behind success, and why performance alone is never enough.

Buy AmazonBuy Local
Most Shareable · 2018 Winner

Rebel Talent

by Francesca Gino

Harvard’s Francesca Gino makes the research-backed case that the most valuable people at work are the ones who break the rules, constructively.

Buy AmazonBuy Local
Most Important · 2018 Winner

Bullshit Jobs

by David Graeber

Anthropologist David Graeber names the phenomenon everyone recognized but no one discussed: jobs that even the people doing them believe are meaningless, and what that does to us.

The Shortlist

Ten shortlist selections.

The finalists — narrowed from the longlist and revealed during the live announcement show.

Atomic Habits
by James Clear

James Clear’s modern classic on how tiny changes compound into remarkable results. The best-selling productivity book of its generation, honored here on our shortlist the year it was published.

Buy AmazonBuy Local
Dear Client
by Bonnie Siegler

Design legend Bonnie Siegler’s cheeky, practical guide for anyone who hires creative people, on how to get the great work you are actually paying for.

Buy AmazonBuy Local
Farsighted
by Steven Johnson

Steven Johnson examines the decisions that take years to play out, and the tools that help us choose wisely when the stakes are highest and the feedback is slowest.

Buy AmazonBuy Local
Never Lose a Customer Again
by Joey Coleman

Joey Coleman shows that customer loyalty is won or lost in the first 100 days, then gives a phase-by-phase playbook for turning any sale into a lasting relationship.

Buy AmazonBuy LocalListen To Episode
Measure What Matters
by John Doerr

Legendary investor John Doerr introduces OKRs, the goal-setting system he brought from Intel to Google, through the stories of the organizations it transformed.

Buy AmazonBuy Local
Parisian Charm School
by Jamie Cat Callan

Jamie Cat Callan’s delightful guide to the French art of charm: presence, conversation and everyday elegance as skills anyone can practice.

Buy AmazonBuy Local
Talk Triggers
by Jay Baer and Daniel Lemin

Jay Baer and Daniel Lemin show how to build operational differentiators that customers cannot stop talking about. Word of mouth, engineered on purpose.

Buy AmazonBuy Local
Technically Wrong
by Sara Wachter-Boettcher

Sara Wachter-Boettcher exposes the biased assumptions built into the apps and algorithms we use every day, and why tech’s blind spots become everyone’s problem.

Buy AmazonBuy Local
The Art of Gathering
by Priya Parker

Priya Parker transforms how we think about meetings, parties and conferences: every gathering needs a purpose, and most of ours have none. Few books change daily life this much.

Buy AmazonBuy Local
When
by Daniel H. Pink

Daniel Pink turns timing into a science: when to work, when to break, when to switch. Proof that when you do things matters as much as what you do.
