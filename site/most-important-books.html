Award Winner Roundups

The Most Important
Non-Fiction Books
Of The Past Decade

Every year since 2014, the Non-Obvious Book Awards have honored one book as the Most Important Book of the Year: the book with the biggest ideas about the world we live in, and the one our judges believe matters most. Collected here: every winner, in one definitive reading list.

*This list does not include books published in 2026, which are currently under review as part of our 2026 awards program.

2025 · Most Important Winner

Moral Ambition

by Rutger Bregman

Rutger Bregman challenges the most talented people of our generation to stop wasting their careers, and redirect their ambition toward the world's biggest problems.

2024 · Most Important Winner

Brave New Words

by Salman Khan

Khan Academy founder Salman Khan offers the most credible early map of how AI will transform how our kids learn, and why that future is more hopeful than we fear.

Buy AmazonBuy LocalAll 2024 Winners →
2023 · Most Important Winner

How Big Things Get Done

by Bent Flyvbjerg and Dan Gardner

Megaproject expert Bent Flyvbjerg distills a lifetime of data on why big projects fail, and the surprising factors behind the rare ones that succeed. Essential for anyone leading anything ambitious.

2022 · Most Important Winner

Stolen Focus

by Johann Hari

Johann Hari investigates why the world stopped paying attention, and shows that our attention crisis is not a personal failing but something being done to us.

2021 · Most Important Winner

The Lonely Century

by Noreena Hertz

Economist Noreena Hertz documents how loneliness became a defining condition of our age, reshaping our health, our economies and our politics, and what it takes to reconnect.

2020 · Most Important Winner

Make Change

by Shaun King

Activist Shaun King draws on his years at the center of modern movements to lay out how outrage becomes organized, durable change.

2019 · Most Important Winner

Secondhand

by Adam Minter

Adam Minter follows our castoffs through the hidden global economy of secondhand goods, revealing what really happens to the things we give away.

2018 · Most Important Winner

Bullshit Jobs

by David Graeber

Anthropologist David Graeber names the phenomenon everyone recognized but no one discussed: jobs that even the people doing them believe are meaningless, and what that does to us.

2017 · Most Important Winner

Give Work

by Leila Janah

Samasource founder Leila Janah argues the surest way to end global poverty is not charity but work, and shows how giving work transforms lives and communities.

2016 · Most Important Winner

Persuadable

by Al Pittampalli

Al Pittampalli makes the counterintuitive case that the willingness to change your mind, being persuadable, is a defining strength of modern leadership.

2014/15 · Most Important Winner

The Confidence Code

by Katty Kay and Claire Shipman

Journalists Katty Kay and Claire Shipman investigate the science of self-assurance, and why closing the confidence gap matters as much as competence for women at work.
