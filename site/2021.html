The 2021
Book Awards

Our most competitive year yet at the time — five winners, ten shortlist selections and a longlist of the 75 best non-fiction books of 2021, chosen from hundreds of titles and announced live in December 2021.

The year's biggest themes: the new world of work, shifting power, coming together as a society, facing an uncertain future, putting yourself first — and ending bias for good.

The Best Non-Obvious
Books of the Year

Most Original · 2021 Winner

When We Cease to Understand the World

by Benjamin Labatut

Benjamín Labatút’s genre-defying account of the scientists whose discoveries brushed against madness. It reads like nothing else in the history of our awards.

Most Useful · 2021 Winner

Think Again

by Adam Grant

Adam Grant’s blockbuster on the power of rethinking: why the smartest people update their views, and how intellectual humility beats being right. A modern classic of useful thinking.

Buy AmazonBuy Local
Most Entertaining · 2021 Winner

Four Lost Cities

by Annalee Newitz

Annalee Newitz tours four vanished metropolises, from Çatalhöyük to Cahokia, to uncover why cities die and what our own urban future can learn from theirs.

Buy AmazonBuy Local
Most Shareable · 2021 Winner

Move

by Parag Khanna

Parag Khanna maps the forces of climate, economics and demographics that will make the next decades the most mobile in human history, and predicts where we will all go.

The Lonely Century

by Noreena Hertz

Economist Noreena Hertz documents how loneliness became a defining condition of our age, reshaping our health, our economies and our politics, and what it takes to reconnect.

The Biggest Book Trends of 2021

Six macro trends.
Eighteen micro-trends.

As part of our judging process, we curate the biggest trends from all non-fiction books that we evaluated throughout the year. Here are the six biggest trends we identified from books published during 2021.

Macro Trend 01

#newwork

One of the six overarching macro trends of 2021 — explored through the three micro-trends below.

#newwork › Micro-Trend 1

#flexiblework

After a year of working virtually, the workplace is becoming more flexible for many people.

#newwork › Micro-Trend 2

#overload

As the way we work evolves rapidly, finding a better balance between work and life becomes key.

#newwork › Micro-Trend 3

#howwework

Disruption leads to introspection and analysis about the dysfunction, nature and future of work itself.

Macro Trend 02

#powershift

One of the six overarching macro trends of 2021 — explored through the three micro-trends below.

#powershift › Micro-Trend 1

#corruptwinners

The corrupt manipulators are winning, and this range of books delves into why and what can be done.

#powershift › Micro-Trend 2

#reclaimcontrol

As power changes hands, these books tell the story of how it is being reclaimed and what it means.

#powershift › Micro-Trend 3

#choice&change

Choice and change continue to be two of the most popular non-fiction book topics for yet another year.

Macro Trend 03

#comingtogether

One of the six overarching macro trends of 2021 — explored through the three micro-trends below.

#comingtogether › Micro-Trend 1

#healingsociety

How do we get along and heal our society? These books propose some intriguing theories and answers.

#comingtogether › Micro-Trend 2

#understandothers

These books help you understand behavior and language to relate to others with more success.

#comingtogether › Micro-Trend 3

#avoidextremism

From rejecting outrage to seeking the truth, these books help you adopt a more balanced perspective.

Macro Trend 04

#uncertainfuture

One of the six overarching macro trends of 2021 — explored through the three micro-trends below.

#uncertainfuture › Micro-Trend 1

#savinghumanity

Save the earth, have more hope and imagine a more positive future with these books.

#uncertainfuture › Micro-Trend 2

#historylessons

Learn from the distant and recent past to understand and create a better future.

#uncertainfuture › Micro-Trend 3

#innovators

Explore the lives and achievements of geniuses and creators in these books that celebrate innovation.

Macro Trend 05

#youfirst

One of the six overarching macro trends of 2021 — explored through the three micro-trends below.

#youfirst › Micro-Trend 1

#prioritizeyou

The path to happiness might start by being able to put yourself first, as these books suggest.

#youfirst › Micro-Trend 2

#innervoice

Conquer your imposter syndrome by leaning into doubt, focusing and giving yourself more confidence.

#youfirst › Micro-Trend 3

#growthmindset

These authors suggest success comes from learning new skills, focusing on the long term and choosing to always get better.

Macro Trend 06

#endbias

One of the six overarching macro trends of 2021 — explored through the three micro-trends below.

#endbias › Micro-Trend 1

#systemsofbias

How can we end systemic bias that holds people back? These powerful books offer real answers.

#endbias › Micro-Trend 2

#creatingequity

Achieving pay equity, ending workplace bias and making the world fairer are what these books focus on.

#endbias › Micro-Trend 3

#takeaction

Going beyond just conversation, these actionable books suggest real solutions to build a more inclusive world.

The Shortlist

Ten shortlist selections.

The finalists — narrowed from the longlist and revealed during the live announcement show.

Backable
by Suneel Gupta with Carlye Adler

Suneel Gupta went from serial rejection to raising millions, then reverse-engineered why: people don’t back ideas, they back people who make them believe.

Buy AmazonBuy LocalListen To Episode
Dirty Work
by Eyal Press

Eyal Press examines the morally troubling jobs society depends on but refuses to see, from drone operators to slaughterhouse workers, and asks who really bears the ethical cost.

Buy AmazonBuy Local
Futureproof
by Kevin Roose

Kevin Roose’s nine rules for being human in the age of automation. Practical optimism for anyone worried about the robots.

Buy AmazonBuy Local
Nine Nasty Words
by John McWhorter

Linguist John McWhorter takes profanity seriously, tracing how our forbidden words evolved and what swearing reveals about the way language and taboo actually work.

Buy AmazonBuy Local
Spite
by Simon McCarthy-Jones

Psychologist Simon McCarthy-Jones explores our strangest motivation: the urge to hurt ourselves just to hurt someone else more, and why spite may secretly serve us.

Buy AmazonBuy Local
Subtract
by Leidy Klotz

Engineer Leidy Klotz on the untapped science of less: why our instinct is always to add, and what becomes possible when we subtract instead.

Buy AmazonBuy Local
The 1619 Project
by Nikole Hannah-Jones

Nikole Hannah-Jones expands her Pulitzer-winning work into a sweeping reframing of American history that placed slavery and its legacy at the center of the national story.

Buy AmazonBuy Local
The World in a Selfie
by Marco D'Eramo

Marco D’Eramo’s provocative examination of the age of tourism, and what the industry that packages authenticity does to the places and people being visited.

Buy AmazonBuy Local
Under a White Sky
by Elizabeth Kolbert

Elizabeth Kolbert reports on the scientists engineering nature to fix the problems we created by engineering nature. Clear-eyed, wry and quietly alarming.

Buy AmazonBuy Local
Useful Delusions
by Shankar Vedantam and Bill Mesler

Hidden Brain host Shankar Vedantam makes the counterintuitive case that self-deception is not always a bug: some illusions hold lives, relationships and societies together.
